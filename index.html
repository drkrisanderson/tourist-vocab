Tourist Vocab: Thai

How to use this app

Pick any lesson, in any order. Each has 10 words, and the most useful words come first.

  • Literal meanings. Some cards also show what a word literally says, such as toilet (literally: room water). Knowing the parts lets you spot them and reuse them in other situations.
  • Accents. A mark shows the tone of your voice. â means start high, then let it fall. ǎ means start low, then let it rise. Marks appear only where two words would otherwise look the same, such as near (glâi) and far (glai).
  • Sentences. The Sentences lessons join words you already know. Their literal meanings show how each word is used. Ending a sentence with mǎi turns it into a yes or no question. To be polite, add krap (men) or ka (women) at the end of any sentence.
  • Alphabet. The Alphabet lessons show a Thai letter first, then its sound and a word you have met that uses it. In the vowel lesson, the dotted circle marks where the consonant goes: a Thai vowel can sit after, before, above or below it. English spellings of Thai names can mislead. “th” is just t and “ph” is just p, so Phuket is said “Poo-get”.
  • Copy the locals. Tap Listen on a card to hear the word, then repeat it aloud. Listen to how local speakers say a word and copy their accent and rhythm. Avoid saying the words in the accent of your own language.

All done!
